# Model, safety and data limitations

These issues were read from the supplied code and docs, not discovered by a real model benchmark.

1. **The fact checker is not a semantics guarantee.** It chiefly detects supported string patterns (numbers, dates, times, email/links/Latin tokens, user-listed names and, since 24 November, days, months and places from short word lists). It can miss numbers written as words, changed descriptions, swapped order, meaning reversals and unlisted names; it may also withhold valid paraphrases.
2. **Training distribution shift.** The candidate travel corpus has short synthetic/formal sentences about tourism. The golden set for the travel booking desk (v3, a draft that is not reviewed yet) contains booking requests, changes, refunds and complaints written as chat messages with many facts; only the template pairs resemble it. The earlier release v2.2 contains customer-service issues across several domains. Performance is unknown until controlled evaluation.
3. **Training source rights.** The Zenodo record and article label CC BY 4.0, while the corpus's embedded README reportedly describes non-commercial restrictions; treat as licence ambiguity. Credit the author, avoid redistribution of raw data, and confirm rights before publishing derived weights or extensive training examples.
4. **Review provenance and freeze.** All 100 team approvals and reciprocal peer reviews were directly confirmed by the leader as having occurred in his presence. Release v2.2 was committed on 20 November 2026 (commit `eb809ee`); the travel-desk release v3 still needs its own commit before model training.
5. **ML evaluation leakage.** AI-authored template examples, prompt exemplars and the originally AI-drafted golden set may share patterns. Keep independent human review, exact/near/fact overlaps, and a final held-out test.
6. **Deployment.** Real adapter training, GGUF export, inference through Ollama and local Streamlit with the model still require end-to-end testing. No notebook execution or live model metrics has been demonstrated.
7. **Safety cases.** Mock-run statuses don't establish real model behavior or high-level semantic correctness. The adversarial set must be tested with the trained model and reviewed by team members.
8. **Missing-information rules.** `src/needs.py` reads words, not meaning. It will miss a request worded outside its rules and will sometimes ask needlessly (for example when the destination is a place outside the word list, or when a general question uses the noun of an act: "هل فيه استرجاع؟" is asked for a booking number). On the draft it does not ask on 7 of the 40 messages that lack a detail; six are complaints or follow-ups about a service in progress and one reports a wrong name on a ticket. A number that directly follows the word for the booking is taken as its reference even when it is an amount, and the check then stays silent. When the staff member chooses to rewrite without asking, the checker does not catch an added period given in words ("الأسبوع القادم") or a place outside its list. It asks only for the date, the destination and the booking reference, not for the departure city or the travellers. The rules and the golden draft have the same author, so the rate measured on the draft is not an independent measurement.
9. **Key facts depend on the labels and the raters.** A fact that nobody labelled is not counted, and "lost or changed" is the judgment of two team members. The automatic match is only a first look.
